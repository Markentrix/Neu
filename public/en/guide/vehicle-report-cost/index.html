What does a vehicle expert report cost and who pays for it?

The most important answer first: after a not-at-fault accident you generally do not have to pay for the report yourself.

By Patric Schneider, Vehicle engineering expert, Master motor vehicle technicianUpdated: October 2026

In short

In a liability case the costs of the report count as part of the damage and are paid by the insurer of the person at fault. For accident damage the fee is often based on the amount of the damage. For valuation, evidence and lease return reports you receive an individual quote in advance.

After a not-at-fault accident

In a liability case the costs of the report count as part of the damage incurred. As a rule the liability insurer of the person at fault pays them. You have the right to commission an independent expert of your choice.

Insurers like to point to a repair estimate and claim that you would have to pay for an expert yourself. In a liability case that is generally not the case. The reasons are given under reasons for an expert report.

For minor damage a report is often not worth it. I will tell you that honestly before you place an order.

After an at-fault accident

If you caused the accident yourself, there is no opposing party who pays for the report. In that case you generally bear the costs yourself. Check beforehand whether your comprehensive cover insurer prescribes its own procedure.

What the fee depends on

For accident damage the fee is often based on the amount of the damage. For valuation, evidence and lease return reports it depends on the effort involved, for example on the type of vehicle and the scope of the documentation.

How to get a quote

Call me or write to me briefly about what it is about. For reports that you pay for yourself you receive an individual quote in advance. An overview of all reports can be found under services.

This article is general information and not legal advice. How a claim is settled in an individual case depends on the circumstances. For legal questions, please consult your lawyer.

Frequently asked questions

Is a report worthwhile for minor damage?

Usually not. For minor damage a repair estimate is often enough. For larger damage, a total loss or disputed liability a report is almost always worthwhile.

Does the insurer sometimes reduce the expert's costs?

Reductions do happen. In that case, get advice from your lawyer.

Do I have to use the insurer's expert?

No. As the injured party you may commission an independent expert of your choice.
